Appendix A

Tom Carhart and Palio Coffee

This profile draws on public sources compiled for the Company, and on Tom's own words where marked.

  1. The founder's own story
  2. Craft
  3. Organic
  4. Ethical sourcing
  5. Freshness
  6. Industry validation
  7. Where it is poured
  8. A mature business
  1. The founder's own story. A veteran of the coffee trade with a vast network in the field. In Tom's own words, he has decades of experience and learned to roast at the hands of Alfred Peet himself.
  2. Craft. Palio's master roaster blends green beans in small batches and roasts them by hand, "the Italian way", watching every batch from first crack to finish, by choice against the automated roasting most of the industry has adopted.
  3. Organic. Palio is USDA Organic certified, and its decaf uses the chemical-free Swiss Water Process.
  4. Ethical sourcing. Organically grown, ethically sourced beans from around the world, including Central America, East Africa and Guatemala.
  5. Freshness. Palio runs its own dedicated roastery in Alameda, near the Port of Oakland, where green coffee arrives.
  6. Industry validation. Cheyenne Young, who had worked with leading Bay Area roasters, called Palio's beans "far superior", switched his Pump Espresso Bar to Palio exclusively, and became an advocate for the brand.
  7. Where it is poured. Palio's coffee is carried by Good Earth Natural Foods, which keeps some of the strictest sourcing standards in grocery; by The Palace Market, Point Reyes Station; by Saltwater Oyster Depot, Inverness; and by Coast Cafe, Bolinas; and Autodesk is a corporate customer.
  8. A mature business. A registered PALIO COFFEE trademark; brand and operating companies kept as separate entities; and a retail cafe, a corporate and wholesale hub and a production roastery, each in its own location.

Partnership agreement in progress.
